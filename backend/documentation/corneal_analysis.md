## 1. Executive Summary & Physics of Corneal Specular Reflection

`corneal_analysis.py` isolates and compares the microscopic specular reflections (**Purkinje images**) in the left and right eyes of a human subject. In an unmanipulated physical photograph, light bouncing off the cornea obeys strict stereoscopic ray optics; in synthetic imagery generated by Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) and Latent Diffusion Models (LDMs), ocular reflections are frequently inconsistent, asymmetric, or physically impossible.

### 1.1 The Optical Physics: The Cornea as an Aspherical Convex Mirror
The anterior surface of the human cornea forms an aspherical convex mirror covered by a smooth, high-refractive tear film ($n \approx 1.336$):
1. **Purkinje Image I (The Corneal Reflection)**:
   * When light strikes the human eye, four optical interfaces create reflections known as Purkinje images (P-I through P-IV).
   * **Purkinje Image I** (anterior corneal tear film) has a Fresnel reflectance of $\approx 2.5\%$, which is over $100\times$ brighter than the internal reflections from the crystalline lens ($<0.02\%$).
   * P-I forms a sharp, high-intensity virtual image of the luminous environment immediately in front of the subject.
2. **Epipolar Geometry & Parallel Ray Approximation**:
   * The human interpupillary distance ($IPD \approx 6.3\text{ cm}$) is infinitesimal relative to the distance to typical indoor/outdoor illuminants ($D \gg 1.0\text{ m}$).
   * Consequently, light rays emanating from environmental sources arrive at both eyes along virtually parallel vectors ($\theta_L \approx \theta_R$).
   * **The Physical Law**: Both eyes *must* view the exact same luminous environment: the specular highlights in the left and right corneas must possess identical geometric shapes, matching internal contours, and corresponding angular positions relative to the corneal apex.

### 1.2 Generative AI & Deepfake Vulnerabilities
* **Absence of 3D Scene Geometry**: 2D GANs (StyleGAN1/2/3, ProGAN) and 2D Diffusion Models (Stable Diffusion, Midjourney, Flux) generate human faces through statistical pixel correlations without rendering a cohesive 3D physical world.
* **Telltale Generative Artifacts**:
  1. **Shape Mismatches**: One eye reflects a square window softbox while the other reflects a circular ring light or diffused ambient smear.
  2. **Angular Discrepancies**: Highlights positioned at 10 o'clock in the left eye and 4 o'clock in the right eye.
  3. **Monocular Glare**: A prominent highlight present in one eye and completely missing from the other.
  4. **Amorphous Spatters**: Irregular, fractal, non-physical light clumps.

## 2. Palpebral Fissure & Eye Contour Extraction

The engine utilizes **MediaPipe FaceLandmarker** to obtain 478 3D facial coordinates normalized to $[0.0, 1.0]$.

### 2.1 Anatomical Contour Landmarks
To eliminate eyelashes, eyelid skin folds, and outer orbital shadows, the engine isolates the palpebral fissure using 16 anatomical boundary vertices per eye:

* **Left Eye** (Subject Left / Image Right):
  $$\mathcal{I}_L = [362, 382, 381, 380, 374, 373, 390, 249, 263, 466, 388, 387, 386, 385, 384, 398]$$
  *(Includes medial canthus 362, lateral canthus 263, upper margin 386/385/384, lower margin 374/373/390).*
* **Right Eye** (Subject Right / Image Left):
  $$\mathcal{I}_R = [33, 7, 163, 144, 145, 153, 154, 155, 133, 173, 157, 158, 159, 160, 161, 246]$$
  *(Includes medial canthus 133, lateral canthus 33, upper margin 159/158/157, lower margin 145/144/163).*

Pixel coordinates are computed as:
$$\mathbf{p}_i = \left( \lfloor x_i \cdot W \rfloor, \lfloor y_i \cdot H \rfloor \right) \quad \text{for } i \in \mathcal{I}$$

### 2.2 Polygonal Masking & Isotropic Bounding
1. **Full-Resolution Polygon Masks**:
   Constructs binary masks $\mathbf{M}_L^{\text{full}}, \mathbf{M}_R^{\text{full}} \in \{0, 255\}^{H \times W}$ via `cv2.fillPoly`.
2. **Padded Bounding Box Cropping**:
   Calculates minimum bounding rectangles with a 5-pixel margin padding:
   $$x_{\min} = \max(0, \min(p_x) - 5), \quad x_{\max} = \min(W, \max(p_x) + 5)$$
   $$y_{\min} = \max(0, \min(p_y) - 5), \quad y_{\max} = \min(H, \max(p_y) + 5)$$
3. **Standardized Resizing**:
   Both eye patches are cropped and standardized to a fixed square dimension:
   $$\text{target\_size} = (64, 64)$$
   * RGB images are resized via **bilinear interpolation**.
   * Polygon masks are resized via **nearest-neighbor interpolation** (`cv2.INTER_NEAREST`) to preserve crisp binary edges without intermediate values.

## 3. Color-Informed Specular Highlight Isolation

Pure RGB thresholding is vulnerable to colored ambient lighting and iris pigmentation. The module converts the isolated ocular RGB patches into the **CIE-L\*a\*b\*** color space to operate on pure physical luminance ($L^*$).

### 3.1 Adaptive Threshold Formulation
1. **Masked Luminance Isolation**:
   $$L^*(x, y) = L^*(x, y) \cdot \mathbb{I}(\mathbf{M}(x, y) > 0)$$
2. **Dual-Criteria Dynamic Cutoff ($\tau$)**:
   To ensure that isolated pixels represent genuine specular reflections rather than light-colored irises or pale sclera:
   $$p_{90} = \text{Percentile}\left(\{L^*(x, y) \mid \mathbf{M}(x, y) > 0\}, 90\right)$$
   $$\tau = \max\left(180, p_{90}\right)$$
   * *Rationale for 180 Clamp*: In dark or shaded settings, $p_{90}$ could be low (e.g. 110). A pure percentile threshold would erroneously classify ordinary sclera or light iris tissue as highlights. The absolute floor of 180 ensures that only high-intensity Fresnel reflections are extracted.
   * *Rationale for $p_{90}$ Dynamic Ceiling*: In bright environments, light-colored eyes could have broad diffuse reflections; $p_{90}$ isolates the sharpest 10% peaks of specular intensity.
3. **Binary Highlight Extraction**:
   $$H(x, y) = \begin{cases} 255 & \text{if } L^*(x, y) \ge \tau \\ 0 & \text{otherwise} \end{cases}$$

### 3.2 Morphological Dilation for Spatial Invariance
Micro-deviations in head yaw or slight MediaPipe landmark jitter can introduce 1–3 pixel translational offsets between the cropped eyes. To provide spatial tolerance, the binary highlight masks are dilated with a $7 \times 7$ square structuring element:

$$H_{\text{dilated}} = H \oplus \mathbf{K}_{7 \times 7}, \quad \mathbf{K}_{7 \times 7} = \mathbf{1}_{7 \times 7}$$

On a $64 \times 64$ patch, a $7 \times 7$ kernel expands boundary contours by up to $3\text{ pixels}$ ($4.7\%$ of ocular crop width), perfectly compensating for nominal stereoscopic disparity without merging distinct light sources.

## 4. Quantitative Geometric Comparison Metrics

The system computes two complementary mathematical indices between the left ($H_L$) and right ($H_R$) eye highlights.

### 4.1 Intersection over Union (IoU)
Evaluated on the dilated highlight masks to measure spatial overlap tolerance:

$$\text{IoU} = \frac{\sum_{x, y} \left( H_{L, \text{dilated}}(x, y) \land H_{R, \text{dilated}}(x, y) \right)}{\sum_{x, y} \left( H_{L, \text{dilated}}(x, y) \lor H_{R, \text{dilated}}(x, y) \right)}$$

* *Edge Case*: If both eyes contain zero detected highlights (e.g. subject wearing dark sunglasses or in pitch darkness), the union is zero, and $\text{IoU}$ is set to $1.0$ (perfect agreement).

### 4.2 Structural Similarity Index (SSIM)
Evaluated on the raw, undilated binary highlight thresholds ($H_L, H_R$) to measure shape, contour, and luminance distribution similarity:

$$\text{SSIM}(H_L, H_R) = \frac{(2\mu_L\mu_R + c_1)(2\sigma_{LR} + c_2)}{(\mu_L^2 + \mu_R^2 + c_1)(\sigma_L^2 + \sigma_R^2 + c_2)}$$
Where constants $c_1 = (0.01 \times 255)^2$ and $c_2 = (0.03 \times 255)^2$.

### 4.3 Combined Similarity & Raw Anomaly
$$\text{Sim}_{\text{combined}} = \frac{\text{IoU} + \max(0.0, \text{SSIM})}{2.0}$$
$$\text{Anomaly} = 1.0 - \text{Sim}_{\text{combined}}$$

## 5. Optical Interference Suppression (False Positive Prevention)

Authentic human subjects frequently wear eyeglasses or stand in side-lit rooms where the light path to one eye is occluded by the nasal bridge. The engine includes an automated suppression engine to prevent false deepfake flags.

```
Total Glare Area A_total = Area(L) + Area(R)
Asymmetry Ratio R_diff = |Area(L) - Area(R)| / max(1.0, A_total)
```

| Interference Scenario | Detection Criterion | Suppression Mathematical Action | Forensic Justification |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Eyeglass Lens Glare** | $A_{\text{total}} > 150\text{ pixels}$ | $\text{Anomaly} \leftarrow \text{Anomaly} \times 0.20$ | Eyeglass lenses produce massive planar reflections spanning entire eye crops, obliterating tiny corneal reflections. Dampens anomaly by **80%**. |
| **Side / Oblique Lighting** | $R_{\text{diff}} > 0.30$ | $\text{Anomaly} \leftarrow \text{Anomaly} \times (1.0 - R_{\text{diff}})$ | Lateral illuminants at $>60^\circ$ are physically occluded from the far eye by the nasal bridge. Dampens anomaly proportionally to geometric shadow ratio. |
| **Dark Scene / No Highlights** | $A_{\text{total}} = 0$ | $\text{IoU} = 1.0 \implies \text{Anomaly} = 0.0$ | No specular highlights exist in either eye; mutually consistent darkness. |

### 5.1 Final Anomaly Scoring Formulation
$$\text{corneal\_score} = \text{clamp}\left(\text{Anomaly} \times 1.5 \times Q, 0.10, 0.95\right)$$
Where $Q \in [0.3, 1.3]$ is the upstream Image Quality Assessment sharpness multiplier.

## 8. Meta-Classifier Integration & Failure Modes

* **Meta-Classifier Vector Position**: Input Feature Index 14 (`corneal_score`) in the PyTorch Tabular ResNet (`ensemble_classifier.py`).
* **Interactions**:
  * Highly complementary to **Feature 9 (`lighting_anomaly_score`)**. When global face vs. background lighting is physically impossible and corneal reflections display mismatched light source geometries, the self-attention gating layer treats this as multimodal confirmation of synthetic face generation.
* **Handled Edge Cases**:
  1. **No Face Detected**: Gracefully aborts with `{"corneal_score": 0.5, "warning": "No face detected...", "corneal_map_path": None}`.
  2. **Closed Eyes / Blinking**: If eyelid contours occlude the cornea, $L^*$ contains zero pixels $\ge 180$, resulting in zero highlights and a neutral score.
  3. **Zero-Area Crops**: Guarded by `if left_eye_rgb.size == 0:` returning a safe fallback.
  4. **Singleton Landmarker Caching**: Reuses `get_landmarker()` from `pipeline/face_geometry.py` to prevent redundant memory allocation.
